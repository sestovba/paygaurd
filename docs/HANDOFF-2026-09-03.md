## 1 · The product was reframed this session. This is the big one.

Sergey, from his own experience of getting SSDI:

> "When I got SSDI I heard about rules and went to gemini to ask questions.
> Then I realized my job search criteria depended on this information. But I
> didnt now all of the variables and what job I should apply for that has the
> right hourly rate and schedule. It's always a part time job. **So our
> simulator is the very first thing a person wants to see its not an
> afterthought.**"

**The app is built as a record keeper with a simulator buried in a settings
sheet. It should be a job-search calculator that later becomes a record
keeper.** Two phases, one hinge question:

| | Phase 1 · **Deciding** | Phase 2 · **Recording** |
|---|---|---|
| When | Before the job exists | Once you are working |
| Asks | What should I even apply for? | Am I about to go over? |
| Gives | Hours a week, an hourly rate, a schedule | A verdict per month |
| Built? | **Barely — see below** | Eight layouts |

The hinge, asked once after onboarding, never in Settings:

> **Are you employed, or actively looking for work?** · I'm working · I'm looking

Two rules that must hold for anything built on this — both are in `CLAUDE.md`
under **"What the product is"**:

1. **It runs both ways.** Somebody looking for work has no rate yet; they have
   a body that can manage a certain number of hours. `capacityFor` already
   does `rate → hours`. **`hours → rate` does not exist anywhere** and is the
   actual job-search criterion.
2. **Every hours figure is derived, never printed as a rule.** "About 10 hours
   a week" is `SAFE_MONTHLY ÷ 4.33 ÷ rate`. It is 10 at $21.50/hr, 15 at $15,
   6 at $35. Hard-coding it hands a low-paid worker a wrong answer in the
   direction that costs money.

Recorded as `task-simulator-is-the-product`.

## 3 · The immediate next task

**Sergey chose "September, Redesigned".** He was interrupted before saying
more, so confirm the target layout before writing much — but the portable
pieces are clear, and they belong in shared code rather than in one screen.

### What the redesign actually contributes

1. **Two linear meters instead of a ring.** A circle implies a full cycle, so
   the eye reads the whole ring as the budget; his version had the limit
   marker at two o'clock with grey arc continuing past it, implying somewhere
   to go beyond $1,210 that is never named.
2. **Hours get their own meter — this is the safety fix.** Over 80
   self-employed hours uses a trial month *whatever* was earned. Today that
   number sits in a text input labelled `S/E HOURS`. A driver can be at $430
   counted — nowhere near the money limit — and lose the month on hours, and
   **no layout warns them before it happens.**
3. **Phase-aware verdict, framed as permission.** During the trial period,
   crossing the limit costs a month, *not* a payment. "September will not use
   a trial month" frames staying small as the win, and someone with months
   left may turn down work they were entitled to take. `plan` already says it
   correctly: *"Earn any amount, keep your payment."*
4. **The receipt is the form** — every line is both the figure and the place
   you change it, instead of a breakdown plus separate inputs repeating it.
5. **One encoding of the nine months**, with the return date (*"July 2029"*)
   rather than the rule (*"five years after it was spent"*).

### What the domain already gives you

`src/domain/capacity.ts` is further along than it looks:

- `capacityFor(data, month)` → `Capacity`, including `hoursRule.worked` and
  `hoursRule.left`, and a `Binding` type that is already `'money' | 'hours'`.
  **The two-ceiling logic exists.** Nothing renders the hours ceiling as a
  meter.
- `selfEmployedHours(data, month)`, `outcomeOf(capacity, extraHours)` for the
  try-a-shift stepper, `toneOf()` for state.
- `TWP_SELF_EMPLOYMENT_HOURS = 80` in `rules.ts`.

So this is largely a **presentation** task, not a domain one. The one genuine
domain addition is `hours → rate` for Phase 1, which is the same equation
solved for the other unknown.

### How to build it — and how NOT to

**⚠ This section was wrong in the first version of this handoff, and a session
acting on it built a theme overlay on top of `payguard`. That work was
reverted. What follows is the corrected instruction.**

**Do not land these pieces in an existing layout.** September Redesigned is
**requirements gathering** — it and the other artifacts exist to get the
individual components right on paper. The output is not a patch to
`SafetyHero` or `payguard`; it is a specification for a **new theme, built
clean from the start**.

Sergey, after the overlay was reverted:

> "September redesign was us gathering requirements and working on the
> individual components to get them right... the final step would have been to
> create a new theme not hack over an existing one, we want a clean, modern new
> design built right from the start."

So the order is: **requirements → implementation strategy → new theme.** The
strategy is the next deliverable, not code. The eight existing layouts are
**not touched** — they are the reference material and the fallback, and
anything bolted onto them is thrown away twice.

What *is* safe to build ahead of the theme is the one genuine **domain** gap,
because `src/domain/` is shared truth rather than a layout:

- `hours → rate` in `capacity.ts` — the same equation `capacityFor` already
  solves, rearranged for the other unknown. Needed by Phase 1 and by nothing
  that exists today.

**Then Sergey corrected the frame:** *"This is not necessarily rank but

| `ledger` | Entry through tabs, and the vibe | workable | **shines** | 89 | 5/8 |

| `payguard` | Desktop editing | **afterthought** | **built for it** | 89 | 5/8 |
| `horizon` | The forward view; doesn't let you act | workable | workable | 90 | 7/8 |

**`pocket` is last on points and is the heart of the product.** That is a flaw
in the instrument, not in pocket — breadth is the wrong measure for a layout
that is deliberately subtractive. Do not make a cut decision from the ranking.

**The split is a viewport split, not a quality split.** Only `calc20` works on
both, which is why "most impressive and smooth" and "brittle and hard to
maintain" are both true of it: **its value is interaction quality, which is
portable; its cost is architecture, which is not.** Take the feel, leave the
`UiState` shim.

Three findings worth acting on:

- **"Answer in hours" is in 2 of 8 layouts**, and the two-way version in none —
  the feature the reframe puts first is barely built.
- **`ledger` and `payguard` — the two best at entry — have no mileage
  deduction and no net→gross.** A driver typing into the fastest surfaces has
  the full payout counted, when $1,000 of delivery pay can be under $300.
  Most expensive gap in the audit, in the most-used path.
- **`plan` and `pocket` both lack the extra-paycheck warning** — the two
  layouts Sergey says "have it right" both omit the commonest way to go over.

## 7 · Rules that catch people out

- **No abbreviations on screen, ever** — including units. TWP, SGA, YTD, hrs,
  mi. The full banned list is `NEVER` in `domain/copy.ts`.
- **One limit at a time.** Name the limit that applies and never the other one.
- **Round down, always.** Wrong-down costs a few hours of pay; wrong-up costs a
  debt to Social Security.
- **The number people have is net.** Ask for what reached the bank, convert out
  loud.
- **Verify in the browser, not the source.** Twice this session a surface was
  called clean off a source scan and the rendered page then found more.
- **Never call visual work done from a passing typecheck.** Open the app.
- Four calculation questions in `WORKING-WITH-SERGEY.md` §3 are unresolved *by
  choice*. Do not change them without a decision from Sergey.
