Crossbreed spec · the ninth layout

If these two had a baby

They look similar and feel different, and the reason is not styling. The two hermes layouts are the two halves of the product, built separately by accident.

The tracker has no jobs, no types and no ledger — a month is a bucket you push numbers into, and its best screen answers how many hours could I work. PayGuard has jobs, types, entries, a ledger and trial months, and its best screen answers am I about to go over.

Those are Phase 1 and Phase 2 in CLAUDE.md, drawn by two people who had not read it. So the child is not a visual blend of two designs. It is one layout that carries both phases, and the hinge between them.

01

The finding

Nightfall · the tracker

No jobs. No types. No deductions. A month is a bucket. Asks: what should I apply for?

289 lines of behaviour. Its one real calculation is limit ÷ rate ÷ 4.33 — the hours answer, live on every keystroke.

Daylight · payguard

Jobs, types, entries, a ledger, trial months, a bank tab. Asks: am I about to go over?

1,627 lines. Its one correct calculation is countedAmount — pay in full for a job, minus costs for gig work.

Neither is a draft of the other. They share almost no surfaces — the tracker's month grid and PayGuard's job cards do not overlap at all — because they were answering different questions. Merging their stylesheets would produce a page that does both jobs badly. Carrying both questions, and asking which one you are in, produces the product.

02

The hinge

One question, asked once, right after onboarding — never buried in settings. It is the only thing that decides which half of the app you see first.

Are you working right now, or looking for work?

You can change this whenever it changes. Nothing you have written down is lost either way.

Why it cannot be a setting. Someone looking for work has no hourly rate yet — they have a body that manages a certain number of hours. Ask them to fill in a job first and the app is useless to them on the day they most need it. The hinge is the difference between a record keeper and a job-search tool, and it costs one question.

Only the first card swaps. Everything below it is identical in both modes — "which months pay me extra" and "how many trial months are left" are questions you have whether or not you have found the job yet.

Pay and Limit are not screens. Press the tabs — they scroll. There is one page, always rendered, so nothing mounts or unmounts, no scroll position is lost, and the back button is never wrong. It also means a reader who never touches the nav still finds the ledger by scrolling past it, which is how most people will meet it.

Still open: the Bank tab. Your PayGuard has a fourth destination for reconciling bank lines against entries. It is not in this spec because I have not audited it — it arrived in the update that overwrote the session's work. It is the one part of the crossbreed I would want to look at before committing to three tabs or four.

05

What is not inherited

Named explicitly, because a refactor would take these along and a rebuild should not.

Left behindWhy
Every calculation in both filesThe gauge divides a year by a monthly limit. The trial tracker counts any month with any entry. "YTD" includes all prior years. src/domain/ answers all three correctly and is shared by eight layouts.
The 20-column ledgerEleven empty cells per row by construction — a matrix drawn as a list. The transaction half comes; the month matrix is the month cells' job.
The atmosphereThree animated 600px gradients repainting continuously. Beautiful, and a battery bill on the device this is for.
Blur, glow, web fontsThe device rule. All four fail silently rather than degrading.
The banned vocabularySGA, TWP, W-2, 1099, gross, YTD, sources, earnings. Three of them can cost the reader money.
The twelve-cell year gridfocusMode exists to remove it. The child shows this month and the ones after it, and offers the year on request.

Nothing here recomputes a benefits rule. Every number comes from capacity.ts, trialWork.ts, paySchedule.ts and earnings.ts. That is most of the saving, and it is also the only way the child can be correct — the prototypes are confidently wrong about the exact things people would rely on.

07

The one thing neither parent has

Hours → rate. "I can manage 10 hours a week — what do I need to be paid?" It is the same equation solved for the other unknown, it is the actual job-search criterion, and it is absent from both prototypes, from all eight layouts, and from src/domain/.

Nightfall's updatePlan is one line away from it, which is why the child inherits from that parent rather than reinventing: limit ÷ rate becomes limit ÷ hours, and the looking mode above fills either box from the other. That is the trait that makes this a job-search tool rather than a ninth way to record income.
