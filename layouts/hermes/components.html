PayGuard components

A design system,
borrowed from hermes

Not a tour of the prototypes — the kit itself. Every trait worth keeping from the two hermes layouts, rebuilt against the rules the product actually has to meet.

What changed in the rebuild: no web fonts, no backdrop-filter, no blurred shadows, no color-mix() — the device rule, so this runs on an old Android WebView. Every touch target is at least 44px. Inputs are 16px so iOS does not zoom on focus. Light and dark are both complete.

The writing is hermes's, in the register the design system already has a name for. Dense surfaces — cards, facts, table headings — speak compact: the shortest complete label, two to four words, no sentence. The entry dialog speaks spoken: questions, one idea a line. That is the shift you feel between the tracker's "Left this month" and its "How close are you?", and it is deliberate rather than inconsistent.

Shortening drops words the surrounding row already supplies. It never drops the word carrying the meaning — a column under a job's name need not repeat you, but a row of miles must still say for work, because personal miles are not deductible.

§

Where this sits

Two authorities already exist and neither is replaced here. docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md owns the words — master vocabulary, the four questions every label answers, the banned list, the three tones. src/styles/palette.css owns colour — 39 choices, five variants, and the rule that every stylesheet is either a bridge or a marked override.

What was missing is the layer between them: how a thing is built. Heights, weights, states, which box owns an emphasis, what a label may not look like. That is what this page is, and every rule below came out of measuring two prototypes rather than out of taste.

The rules

Eight, each one earned by something that went wrong in hermes. A component that breaks one of these is wrong even if it looks right.

RuleBecause
One control height, one control type size.--control-h 44px and --control-text 15px. Anything sharing a row shares both, so a row of mixed things sits on one baseline.
Weight goes up as you go in.A section stays at 1px however deep the nesting. The card being edited takes the 2px ring — the box that holds it never competes with the box you opened.
An emphasis may not change layout.Rings are drawn inside with outline-offset:-2px. Swapping 1px for 2px grows the box and shifts every row in it the moment you open it.
A label may not look like a control.Carried by fill, not size: solid and borderless against outlined and transparent. Same height is fine; same treatment is not.
Colour is never the only carrier.Every month cell says left, over or all of it in words. It has to survive being printed, dimmed, or read by someone who cannot separate the greens from the reds.
Missing is not zero.An em dash means a real zero; italic words mean we were never told. A month with no earnings is safe; a month we cannot check is not.
Explain at the point of doubt, never in advance.Hints under the field, plain-words titles on the control, the consequence beside the button. No tour, no help icons, no tooltip library.
One thing moves.The pulse on the month you are in is the only animation. Give a second thing motion and the first stops meaning now.

And one that is not ours to break. No color-mix(), no backdrop-filter, no web fonts, no blurred shadows — the device rule in CLAUDE.md. Every one of those fails silently rather than degrading, which is the worst way for a thing to fail on a phone you cannot test on.

The answer waits in place. Before you type a rate, both slots already show — hours in the size and position the real answer will take. The panel never changes shape when it fills in, so the reader is not surprised by a layout that grows under their thumb — and the unit is there from the start, so the number arrives meaning something.

What all eight have in common: the rule is stated where the number is. Not in a tooltip, not behind "How it works", not in an onboarding step — on the line underneath the figure it governs. That is why the hermes screens read as calm despite carrying a benefits rulebook, and it is the single most transferable thing in the copy.

What had to change, and why it is not negotiable. SGA, TWP, Trial Work Period, W-2, 1099, Gross, YTD, Earnings and Sources are all on the never list in src/domain/copy.ts. Three of them can cost the reader money: a driver who does not recognise "1099" cannot know the mileage deduction is theirs, and "gross" is the number almost nobody can find. The banned words are exactly the ones that make these lines sound authoritative — which is the trap, because sounding authoritative to someone who already knows the jargon is not the job.

02

Card and section head

.card · .card__head · .card__label · .card__question

Flat fill, one hairline, no shadow — elevation is a surface step. The head has exactly two sides: everything descriptive on the left, the one action on the right. Anything added goes on the left, or the action drifts into the middle.

From: PayGuard's section head, and the tracker's question-as-subtitle. Fixed: the count now sits with the label. In hermes it was a third child of a space-between row, which pushed the button into the centre.

The dot. Six pixels, brand violet, one per section — and it is the entire decorative budget of the layout. Everything else on the page is doing a job; this is the only mark that exists to give the eye a place to start. That is why it is worth keeping and why it must stay meaningless: the moment it turns amber on a section that needs attention it becomes a signal, and then every section without one is making a claim too. A rhythm mark and a status light cannot be the same 6 pixels.

03

Buttons

.btn--primary · .btn--quiet · .btn--danger · .btn--sm

Three intents, all built on a 1px border so a quiet button and a loud one occupy identical space. 44px minimum, always — hermes had an 11px control at 3px padding.

Changed: hover is a darker shade, never a shadow. Press is a 1px drop. Focus is a real visible ring — neither prototype had one.

04

Pay day field

.input--day · sanitiseDay()

A pay day is a day of the month and nothing else. No calendar, no dropdown, no month picker — two digits you type. The month is stated beside the field as a fact, because the entry is already filed under one.

Enforced as you type: digits only, two at most, 1 to 31, never 0 and never 32. Leading zeros are stripped. A field that will not accept a wrong character never has to tell anyone they were wrong.

Try to break it. Letters do nothing, three digits will not fit, 0 will not stick, 32 keeps only the 3. Changed: left-aligned like every other field, and the label says Pay day everywhere — hermes called the same value "Day", "Payday" and "Anchor payday (day of month)" on three screens.

05

Job card

.job · .chip--parttime · .chip--gig · .pill

Name, then the kind of work, then status. The chip sits directly against the pills, so the two read as one cluster on the right — what kind of work this is, and whether you are doing it now. Two facts of the same kind, answering together, with the name left alone as the identifier.

Status is three visible pills, one tap, no menu, and each carries a plain-words title on hover.

Everything in the row is one height and one type size — --control-h 44px, --control-text 15px. Buttons, inputs, pills and the chip all read off those two tokens, so a row of mixed things still sits on one line with one baseline.

The chip is still not a control, and the difference is carried by fill instead of size: solid and borderless against outlined and transparent. That is the distinction that survives being the same height — sizing a label down to prove it is not a button just makes the row ragged.

Changed: "W-2" and "1099" are on the banned list, so the chips read Part time and Gig work — both named for what the reader does rather than what the tax form is called. Fixed: both selects show the stored value. In hermes they were given a value attribute before their options existed, so every job displayed the first option — every job read "W-2" and "Weekly" whatever was stored. And the hint is real here: hermes promised Esc with no Escape handler anywhere in the file.

The keyboard hint is 16px, and that is deliberate. In hermes it asks for 11 and renders at 16 — the code passes fontSize to setProperty, which only takes kebab-case, so the request was silently dropped and it inherited body size. I first called that a bug and shrank it to the 11 the author intended. Wrong call: 11px is the size the rest of this audit keeps flagging as too small for this reader, and an instruction nobody can read is not a quiet instruction, it is an absent one. The accident was better than the intent, so the kit keeps the accident and means it.

Borrowed: hermes's Rate ($/hr) and Rate ($/mo) — a shared noun with the unit in brackets. Two fields that measure one thing at different scales read as a pair rather than as two unrelated boxes, and the eye picks the unit without re-reading the noun. Changed: the unit is spelled out, because abbreviations are banned outright — and $/hr is the exact case the rule exists for.

One caution on the pattern. It only works when the two really are the same quantity at a different scale. In hermes the second Rate was a monthly planning figure — not a rate at all, and read by no calculation in the file — so the shared noun was hiding a difference instead of exposing one. Here they are an hourly rate and its weekly equivalent, which is a genuine pair, and the product's own rule says it should also run backwards: type the week and the hour follows.

07

Add card

.add · .add__echo

Same action as the button in the section head, and it does not read as a repeat: big and muted against small and filled, dashed against solid, bottom against top. The primary button is the control; this is the invitation.

Then the copy closes it — it names its own duplicate out loud, so a reader about to wonder "is this the same button?" is answered before they ask.

08

Month cells

.months · .month__left

They count down, not up. An untouched month reads $1,210, not $0. One line of display logic, and it changes what the grid is for — a row of zeros is a record, a row of ceilings is a plan.

Changed: the colour is never the only carrier — every cell says left, over or all of it in words, so it survives being printed, dimmed, or read by someone who cannot separate the greens from the reds.

09

Trial months

.trial__slot--before|used|now

Nine slots, four states, and a sentence underneath doing the explaining. The graphic never has to be learned.

Trial work months

You have used 5 of your 9 trial work months. Two of those were before you started here. After the ninth, months where you earn more than $1,690 can stop your payments.

Added: the grey "before you started here" state. Hermes styled it and never emitted it — and it is the state that makes the tracker honest for anyone not starting from zero.

Borrowed: the pulse on the month you are in. It is the only animation in the kit, and that is what makes it work — the thing that means now is the only thing allowed to move, so it needs no label and competes with nothing. Hermes has exactly one keyframe animation too, on exactly this. Changed: the ring is spread-only, so it stays a hard edge and survives the no-blurred-shadows rule, and it stops entirely under prefers-reduced-motion. Hermes also puts a 6px blurred glow on the used slots — that part does not come with us.

Not told yet

.job__fact--none · .num--none · .notice__unknown

Missing is a value, and the kit says it one way. Hermes had three on adjacent lines of the same row — -- for a rate, not set for a pay day, — for hours — so a reader could not tell whether the three meant the same thing.

The rule here: an em dash means a real zero. Italic words mean we were never told. They are different facts and they must not look alike — a month with no earnings is safe, a month we cannot check is not.

12c

Top bar

.topbar · .brand · .promise

Brand left, one promise right — and no navigation at all. The most valuable strip on the screen spends itself saying where the data lives.

That is the right call for this reader specifically. Someone deciding whether to type their benefits income into an app has exactly one question first, and it is not "where do I go next".

PayGuard
Stays on this phone · nothing is sent anywhere

From: the tracker's "🔒 Local only · nothing leaves this device". Changed: the padlock is an inline SVG rather than an emoji — emoji rasterise differently on every Android build and some render as a blank box. And it is stated in the reader's terms: "local only" is developer wording for the same promise.

The tracker also repeats this in the footer, in full words. First thing seen and last thing seen say the same thing, which for a promise is worth the duplication.

13

Tab bar

.tabs · .tab

It scrolls rather than routes — one page, always rendered, so nothing mounts, nothing unmounts, no scroll position is lost and the back button is never wrong.

One word each. Hermes gets this right and my first pass did not — I had "What you were paid" and "Your limit" in a tab, which wraps to two lines on a phone and turns a bar of destinations into a paragraph. A tab is a place, not a sentence: the word only has to be different from the other three, because the icon and the screen it lands on carry the rest.

Changed: 52px targets and 13px labels, up from 10px uppercase. The active state is colour and a 3px underline, so it is not carried by hue alone.

—

What is deliberately not here

No gauge, and no arithmetic of our own. The gauge in hermes divides a year of earnings by a monthly limit, so it pins to full by February; its trial tracker counts any month with any entry, with no earnings test and no sixty-month window. Those are not components to borrow — they are questions src/domain/ already answers — capacity.ts, trialWork.ts, paySchedule.ts.

This kit is the interface half. Wire it to the domain that exists rather than reimplementing arithmetic about someone's benefits.
