And that one figure is the same number already printed three cells to its left, in the Gross column. Every amount appears twice on its own row.

So the instinct is right and it is not about width. A 20-column table where 11 cells per row are always empty, one is a duplicate, and one is a restatement is a matrix drawn as a list. The transaction list is good and finished. The matrix wants to be a separate, much smaller thing — which is what Month columns (11) already is.

Pay day field ♥

.pay-day-input · sanitizeDay() · readDay() · dayInput()

A pay day is a day of the month and nothing else. Not a calendar, not a dropdown, not a date picker — two digits you type. That is the whole cognitive load argument: "the 15th" is a thing people know about their own job without looking anything up, and a calendar makes them find a specific square to say it.

The rule, enforced as you type rather than checked on save: digits only, two at most, 1 to 31, never 0 and never 32. Leading zeros are stripped, so 05 becomes 5 and 0 cannot be entered at all. Typing 3 then 2 leaves 3 rather than accepting 32 and then rejecting it. A field that will not accept a wrong character never has to tell anyone they were wrong.

It is also stored as a day. An entry already knows the year and month it is filed under, so composing those back into a 2026-09-07 string only to parse them out again on every read added a round trip and two failure modes for one number the reader typed. The entry carries payDay: 7. Sorting is subtraction, display is a lookup, and nothing parses anything.

Pay cycle
Pay dayof the month
Not toldblank is a real answer — see 19

Try to break the first field. Letters do nothing, three digits will not fit, 0 will not stick, and anything over 31 is refused a digit at a time.

Verified against the live file: "0"→blank, "00"→blank, "05"→5, "007"→7, "abc"→blank, "1a"→1, "32"→3, "35"→3, "99"→9, "345"→3, "30"→30, "31"→31.

A plain text input with inputmode="numeric" — brings up the number pad on Android without type="number"'s spinners and locale grief.One rule in one place; the anchor payday and the entry pay date both go through it.Was max: 28 in the source. Now 31 — which means a job paid on the 30th can be entered, and February has no 30th. The derivation already clamps short months, so nothing breaks, but "the 31st" will silently mean "the 28th" twice a year and the field does not say so.

Extra paycheck warning

.paycheck-warn · extraPaycheckWarnings() · paydayMonthCounts()

Two jobs paid on different days is not a contradiction to resolve. It is the ordinary case, and each job drops its extra cheque in a different month. So nothing reconciles them into one answer — every month that any ongoing job overpays in gets its own row, naming the job that causes it.

The second half matters more than the first. A month we cannot check is not a safe month, and saying nothing about it reads as one. If the reader never told us when a job pays, that job is named as unchecked rather than quietly assumed.

Months with an extra paycheck
March pays you an extra time from Riverside Cafe. That month can go over without you working any differently.
August pays you an extra time from Riverside Cafe. That month can go over without you working any differently.
October pays you an extra time from DoorDash. That month can go over without you working any differently.
We cannot check Instacart yet. Add the day of the month you get paid and we will tell you which months pay you an extra time.

Three months, two jobs, no attempt to pick a winner. March and August come from one job's fortnightly cycle; October from another's. All three are shown. The rule under the hairline is the dependency being stated out loud rather than defaulted away.

Flat --careful-soft fill with its matching text tone — the semantic triple from 01 doing its job.The unknown row is the point. The first version of this defaulted a missing pay day to the 15th, which made every job look answerable and printed invented months for jobs nobody had described.Monthly and semi-monthly jobs can never produce an extra month, so they are silently excluded. Correct, but the reader is not told why their monthly job never appears here.

Typical hours question

hoursMatter() · HOURS_MATTER_ABOVE · state.typicalWeeklyHours

Hours are optional on a W-2 job — SSA counts the money, not the clock — and only half-required on 1099, where the 80-hours-a-month test can matter. So rather than showing an optional box to everyone forever, ask once, in the person's own terms, and stop showing it to anyone whose answer means it can never change their result.

80 hours a month is about 18.5 a week, so the threshold is 18. Someone who typically works well under that cannot reach the test and the field is pure friction for them. Unanswered shows the field — silence is not a low number.

Interactions

scrollIntoView · .modal-overlay.open · .toast.show · .job-edit-panel

The parts that are not markup. This kit has eighteen transitions and exactly one keyframe animation, and everything moves in 100–200ms. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing fades in on load. That restraint is the reason it reads as an application rather than a page — and it is a trait, as much as any card is.

How things move

NavscrollIntoView smoothOne page, always rendered. The nav moves you, it does not swap what exists.

Toastopacity + translateY(8px), 200msSame distance and duration as the modal — one vocabulary of movement, not two.

Now real — the hint used to be lying

"Press Enter to save, Esc to cancel" had no Escape handler anywhere in the file. The most confident piece of micro-copy in the layout was the one instruction that did nothing — and Escape did not close a modal either.

One document-level listener now does all four. Verified live:

escClosedModal: true
enterSaved: true → payDay 9, amount 300, saved and closed
enterClosed: true
escClosedPanel: true

Enter submits the dialog, except from a textarea (where Enter is a newline) and except when focus is on a button (where the reader has tabbed to Cancel and means Cancel). Escape closes the dialog; with no dialog open it closes the job edit panel, exactly as the hint promises.

Found on the second sweep
The Add Job button was in the wrong place, and it was a bug. .section-head is justify-content: space-between, and every other head in the layout has exactly two children so the second lands on the right edge. This one grew a third — the "2 jobs" count — which shoved the button into the middle of the row. Fixed by grouping the count beside the label it counts; the button now lines up with "How it works" in the section above it.

The whole card becomes the editor, and says so with its border. Brand violet ring around the card, type badge filled solid, and the form on a grey inset underneath — so the thing being edited and the form editing it are one object, not a dialog floating over a list.

One panel open at a time (expandedJobId is a single id, not a set), the name turns into an input in the header without shifting, and the hint at the bottom is itself the close button.

Ports as-is. Border and a background step — no shadow, no overlay, no modal. Ring the object being edited — carry this rule anywhere an inline editor opens. Both dropdowns used to lie. A <select> cannot take its value from an attribute set before its options exist, so Type and Cycle always displayed their first option — every job read as "W-2 (counts in full)" and "Weekly" whatever was stored, contradicting the header two lines above. Type is the field that decides whether mileage comes off. Fixed in el(); all eight fields now match the stored job. Count and Rate ($/mo) are in this panel and are read by no calculation in the file. Two of the eight rows collect nothing. Two rows both labelled "Rate" — one hourly, one monthly. They are different quantities wearing the same word.
