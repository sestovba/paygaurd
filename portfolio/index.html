01 — Modernization

Amazon Web Services
Educate

Client Amazon Web Services · Agency Fiduciary Tech
Role Design Consultant · Team AWS Educate
When Feb – May 2025

Design system unification

AWS's education programs — Educate, re/Start and Academy — were built independently over several years. Learners moving between them hit different navigation, different visual language, and different levels of accessibility. It felt like three products, not one ecosystem.

The decision I made

Build one shared UX foundation, not three separate redesigns. Align layouts, navigation and content patterns to Skill Builder standards — so each program keeps its purpose but stops paying the cost of being different.

Flow efficiency up 15%, engagement up 10%, customer satisfaction up 15% — inside a single quarter.

AT&T
Order Lookup & Status

Client AT&T · Agency Axelon
Role Senior UX Designer · Team Cart & Checkout
When Jan – Dec 2022

Post-purchase experience

The moment after someone pays is where trust is most fragile. I owned the order confirmation email and the order status lookup, for both guest and signed-in customers — two audiences whose journeys kept diverging into different-looking screens.

The decision I made

Standardise the components and the messaging hierarchy first, then fix the handoff between email and web — so the journey stops feeling like a black box halfway through.

Mobile usability up 20% through purposeful motion and responsive layouts; design-to-development handoff velocity up 25% off the back of a Figma sub-design-system with shared components and tokens.

03 — Modernization

Optum
StepWise PLM

Client Optum · Agency TEKsystems
Role Senior UX Designer · Team StepWise
When May 2018 – Oct 2019

Regulated healthcare SaaS

StepWise is a web tool with an unusual shape: the people using it are actuaries, underwriters, compliance and product managers who think in spreadsheets — but what the tool produces is a website. Brokers and employers land on it to work out what each employee actually pays for health coverage.

The decision I made

Don't take the spreadsheet away — they're fluent in it, and fighting that mental model was never going to work. Instead, put the output next to the input: render the broker-facing site live beside the grid, so every cell edit has a visible consequence in the thing the customer will see.

Customer satisfaction up 15%, task completion up 10%, friction in the onboarding and customization wizards down 30%.

How I think about design

Three things that shape every one of the specimens above.

01

Systems, not screens

I don't design pages. I design systems that happen to produce pages. The AWS specimen is the whole argument in one toggle — the "after" state isn't prettier, it's the same, and that's the point.

02

Craft survives handoff

If the design can't survive engineering, it isn't finished. Figma deeply — auto layout, variants, tokens — and then HTML, CSS, JavaScript and React to carry it through. This page is that claim, executed rather than asserted.

03

Evidence over opinion

Usability testing, journey mapping, analytics, competitive analysis. I let the numbers argue with my first draft — and where they mattered, I measured them: usability, task success, satisfaction, handoff speed.
