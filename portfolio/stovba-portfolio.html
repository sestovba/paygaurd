How I Think About Design

A few things that shape the work.

Systems, not screens

I don't design pages. I design systems that happen to produce pages. Typography, color, hierarchy, iconography, and grids should hold together across the whole product — not just on the one screen in the mockup.

Craft survives handoff

If the design can't survive engineering, it isn't finished. I work deeply in Figma — auto layout, variants, tokens — and I can carry a concept into HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and React without the intent getting lost on the way.

Evidence over opinion

Usability testing, journey mapping, analytics, competitive analysis. I let the work and the numbers argue with my first draft — and I've measured the results in usability, task success, and customer satisfaction where it mattered.

The through-line: whether the engagement is a 0-to-1 prototype or a multi-year enterprise modernization, the question I'm answering is the same — how do we make this product coherent, credible, and maintainable when the content gets messy and the teams get big.

Case Study · Decision Story

Amazon (AWS) — Educate Modernization

Client: Amazon Web Services · Agency: Fiduciary Tech · Role: Senior Product / UX Designer · Team: AWS Educate

The situation

AWS's education programs — Educate, re/Start, and Academy — were created independently over several years. The result was fragmented learner experiences: different navigation, different visual language, different levels of accessibility and responsiveness. For learners moving between programs as their skills grew, the platforms felt like separate products, not one coherent ecosystem.

The problem

Learners often move between AWS education programs as their skills grow, but the platforms felt disconnected. Key challenges included:

  • Inconsistent UI patterns, spacing, and visual language
  • Different navigation models and information architectures
  • Varying levels of accessibility and responsiveness
  • A perception that each program was a separate product rather than part of a cohesive ecosystem

This fragmentation increased cognitive load and reduced confidence, especially for early-career and non-traditional learners.

The decision I made

Build one shared UX foundation, not a fresh redesign of each program. I aligned layouts, navigation, and content patterns to Skill Builder standards, standardized typography, spacing, and interaction models, and reduced cognitive load while preserving each program's unique purpose — creating a scalable foundation for future enhancements without requiring full platform rewrites.

Impact
A cohesive ecosystem A modernized learning ecosystem across AWS education programs — visually and functionally unified without wiping out each program's identity.
Better transitions Improved usability and consistency for learners moving between offerings — less re-learning, less confusion.
Stronger alignment Closer alignment with AWS's broader design language and accessibility standards — a foundation future teams can build on.

AT&T — Order Lookup & Status Modernization

Client: AT&T · Agency: Axelson · Role: Senior Product / UX Designer · Team: Cart & Checkout

The situation

I supported AT&T's post-purchase experience by improving the order confirmation email and order status lookup flows for both guest and signed-in users. This is the moment after someone has paid — where trust is most fragile and clarity matters most.

The problem

Customers needed clear, trustworthy updates about their orders, while AT&T required strict adherence to brand, accessibility, and design-system standards across email and web. Inconsistencies in messaging and UI patterns created confusion and reduced confidence during the post-purchase journey.

The decision I made

Standardize the UI components and messaging hierarchy first, then fix the handoff between email and web. I improved status indicators and responsive layouts, ensured alignment with AT&T's enterprise design system across touchpoints, and created a seamless transition between email and web experiences — so the post-purchase journey stops feeling like a black box.

Impact
Clearer tracking Increased clarity and customer confidence during order tracking — the post-purchase experience felt less like a black box.
Scalable patterns More consistent, scalable UI patterns across post-purchase surfaces — so future updates don't start from zero.
Better handoff A smoother, more intuitive handoff between email and web — fewer dead zones in the experience.

Case Study · Decision Story

Optum — StepWise Modernization

Client: Optum · Agency: TekSystems · Role: Senior Product / UX Designer · Team: StepWise

The situation

I worked on Optum's StepWise Product Lifecycle Manager (PLM), an enterprise platform that centralizes plan and benefit information for health plans. PLM is the single source of product truth for product managers, actuaries, underwriters, compliance teams, and sales — a lot of people, a lot of data, a lot of stakes.

The problem

Health plans relied on fragmented tools and manual processes to manage product definitions, benefit rules, compliance checks, and plan customization. That created slow time-to-market, inconsistent data, and high operational overhead across the product-to-quote-to-cash lifecycle.

The decision I made

Redesign the workflows around the people who use them every day, not around the tools they inherited. I streamlined product and benefit configuration flows, improved usability of dense data tables, rule engines, and Excel-based tools, enhanced product visualization and workflow transparency, and aligned the experience with Optum's enterprise design system — so the platform guides people toward the right answer instead of making them fight it.

Impact
Faster cycles Faster product development cycles and improved speed-to-market across a complex, regulated platform.
Greater accuracy Greater accuracy through clearer validation and centralized data — fewer errors, fewer manual fixes.
Stronger compliance Stronger compliance via more intuitive rule-building patterns — the system guides people toward the right answer.
Cross-team alignment Improved collaboration across product, actuarial, underwriting, and sales teams — one source of truth, read the same way.

Capital One Investments Modernization

Client: Capital One · Agency: Pyramid Consulting · Role: Senior Product / UX Designer · Team: Web & App Design

The situation

I collaborated with a major U.S. financial institution to explore and validate new concepts aimed at improving the personal banking experience. The work focused on early-stage research, ideation, and prototyping for unreleased features.

Confidentiality note: Due to the sensitive nature of this engagement, specific features, flows, and visual designs cannot be shared. This case study describes process and contribution without revealing proprietary information.

The problem

As customer expectations evolved, the bank sought to reimagine how digital banking tools could become more intuitive, proactive, and personalized — while meeting strict regulatory, security, and accessibility requirements.

The decision I made

Lead with research and low-fidelity prototypes before any commitment to build. I synthesized research to identify unmet customer needs, mapped opportunity areas within the personal banking journey, created low-fidelity prototypes to test early hypotheses, designed neutral, non-branded UI patterns for usability evaluation, and partnered with product, engineering, and compliance teams to validate feasibility — so the team could say what resonated and what didn't before writing production code.

Impact
Validated direction Identified which concepts resonated most with customers — before committing to build.
Clarified problem space Clarified the problem space to guide future product strategy — less guessing, more knowing.
Reusable patterns Established reusable UX patterns for ongoing experimentation — a foundation, not a one-off.
Cross-functional alignment Strengthened alignment across design, product, engineering, and compliance teams — everyone working from the same frame.

AmenPrayers — New App

Self-directed · Independent project · Role: Visual Design & Prototyping

Why this exists

AmenPrayers is a personal exploration — a new app concept designed and prototyped from the ground up. It exists because independent work matters: it's the space where I take risks, test new patterns, and keep my craft sharp between client engagements.

What I'm practicing here

Starting from zero to prove I can still hold the fundamentals. No inherited brand system, no enterprise design language, no one telling me what to do. I get to think carefully about visual hierarchy, typography, color, and responsive grids from scratch — the things that get skipped when you're fitting a design into an existing system.

The app, inside the case study

This is not a screenshot — it's a working specimen of the AmenPrayers app. Tap a card to open the detail, ★ to bookmark, the filter row to narrow the feed, and New to write a prayer from zero. Loading state and undo toast are real too. Same system I'd ship in a client engagement; here it's just inside the portfolio so you can click it instead of judging a still.

Impact
Craft practice A personal space to test new patterns and keep the craft sharp between client engagements.
Visual fundamentals Visual hierarchy across content-dense and content-light states; typography that feels distinctive without sacrificing readability; a color system that carries meaning; responsive grids that hold up from phone to desktop.
The page as proof Instead of asking you to believe the system holds up, this case study renders a working slice of it inline — the same discipline, made clickable.

Process

How I work.

1

Discover

Understand the product, the users, the constraints. Stakeholder alignment, research synthesis, competitive and internal audits.

2

Define Direction

Establish typography, color, hierarchy, and grid decisions early — so the system has room to breathe across the product, not just one screen.

3

Build the System

Reusable components, design tokens, and component libraries that hold up across teams and breakpoints. Designed to survive handoff.

4

Prototype & Validate

Low- and high-fidelity prototypes tested with users. Refined against findings and carried toward production — not abandoned at mockup stage.
